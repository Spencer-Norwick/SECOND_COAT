## Concept

SECOND COAT is a new graffiti method in Mayaford that baits the city into turning cleanup/abatement efforts into a medium of its own. Participants place small, selectively perceptible “seeds” and the city responds by covering these unauthorized markings with cleaning solvents, primer fields, replacement panels, barricades, and work-order overlays. Then, SECOND COAT writers sample the geometry, timing, overlays, and residue of that official response in “chained” tags, sometimes with taunting commentary. They call a completed cleanup a **“muni co-sign”** because the city has unwittingly helped write, enlarge, legitimize, and maintain the message. The traffic jam is that graffiti artists exploit overlay-specific artifacts as a new medium for vandalism and communication. The city can't ignore them and by acting they become participants and provide fodder to the unknown criminals. 

The animated documents formally perform the perceptual effect of transitioning between authorized overlays. An overlay-privileged MPD bulletin resolves as a harmless civic maintenance notice unless the viewer has authorized credentials and a cleared Kapala-Halo. 

## Submission explainer

SECOND COAT draws on Mayaford, the Zoo’s observer-specific overlays, Kapala-Halo access, “Perception Must Preserve,” and the city’s districts and visibility subcultures to turn civic maintenance into a contested communication layer.

The **stigmergy** is the indirect feedback loop: writers leave small perceptible seeds; the city alters the environment in response; other writers read that response and extend it through chained tags. No one has to coordinate the whole sequence because each trace becomes the prompt for the next action.

The **traffic jam** is a new AR-native art form and subversive communication medium that sprung up out of the graffiti subculture of Mayaford. The city cannot ignore the vandalism, but every official cleaning generates new geometry, residue, and civic overlays for SECOND COAT practitioners to appropriate and remix. The abatement system runs to protocol/procedure and still becomes an unwitting co-author and distribution channel of unauthorized signaling.
