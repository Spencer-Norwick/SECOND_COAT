- **Core canon** — established in a published Zoothesia story.

- **Story language** — a phrase used in canon, but not necessarily an official institutional term.

- **Continuity caution** — a spelling or factual inconsistency exists in the published material.

# I. The irreducible rules of Zoothesia

## The Zoo / Zoothesia Protocols

**Type:** protocol/system
**Status:** Core canon; Toybox canon; Crossover canon

A complex, centralized harm-prediction system threaded through ubiquitous augmented-reality overlays. It models whether an observer is likely and able to harm a person or thing, then controls what that observer can perceive. What it hides remains materially present. The system is not usually described as conscious, but it is adaptive, distributed, opaque, and increasingly difficult to distinguish from an agent. [Z0, Z1–Z6, T1, C]

In later canon, the Zoo:

- consumes visual, neural, sensory, and bodily indicators;
- separates fantasy from probable action;
- can hide people, animals, objects, surfaces, sound, touch, smell, and eventually entire social worlds;
- groups people into incomprehensible perceptual strata;
- creates and compares cloned populations in intersecting, mutually invisible subworlds;
- remains governed by its original preservation rule even as its method reverses from mandatory visibility to protective erasure. [Z3, Z6]

**Artifact use:** “The Zoo,” “Zoothesia Protocols,” “Zoo classifications,” “Zoo cohorts,” and observer-specific visibility are secure canon. “Zoo salience logs” and a named municipal Zoo office are plausible extensions, not established institutions.

## Perception Must Preserve

**Type:** governing rule
**Status:** Core canon; Toybox canon

The Zoo’s formal, ironclad founding rule. Initially interpreted as requiring every living thing to remain visible, it evolves into the opposite practical policy: perception itself must not cause harm, so the system hides what an observer might injure. This distinction is foundational. [Z0, Z1, Z6, C]

## Presence Must Preserve

**Type:** colloquial slogan/rule variant
**Status:** Core canon

A genuine everyday slogan used in Chapters 2 and 4. It is distinct from the formal **Perception Must Preserve**, not merely a transcription error. The Jamverse devlog explicitly preserves both. [Z2, Z4, C]

## Overlays

**Type:** device layer / infrastructure
**Status:** Core canon; Toybox canon; Crossover canon

Deep augmented reality that alters sight, sound, touch, and other sensory inputs. Overlays can change weather, clothing, music, faces, architecture, salience, and the perceptibility of living beings. They record experience, display system prompts, and provide pathways by which the Zoo reads neural and physiological activity. [Z0–Z6, T1, C]

**Important limitation:** In Mayaford, characters have considerable aesthetic control but cannot simply mute or erase living beings at will. The Zoo retains control over protective erasure. [Z1, Z2]

## Erasure / withdrawal

**Type:** system action
**Status:** Core canon; Story language

The Zoo’s removal of a materially existing person, object, or signal from a particular observer’s perception. Canon uses **erase**, **hide**, **withdraw**, **subtract**, **render**, and **occlude** in overlapping ways. [Z1–Z6, T1]

**Artifact use:** “masked,” “below civilian salience thresholds,” and “perceptible to selected cohorts” fit the system, but the canon’s most characteristic verbs are **hide**, **erase**, **withdraw from perception**, and **resolve**.

# VI. Body modification, subcultures, and street language

## Faceting

**Status:** Core and Toybox canon

Embedding gemstones, crystalline lattices, and complex refractive structures into the body. Early practitioners hoped optically complex surfaces would resist smooth overlay replacement. Later, faceting becomes a mixture of art, self-harm, religion, and adversarial input: the Zoo expends too many resources reconciling contradictory classifications and elects to “Erase to be Safe.” [Z0, Z3, Z5, T1, C]

Canon says:

- **faceter** — a practitioner;
- **faceters** — the community;
- **final form** — a faceter permanently embedded within a large rock, becoming a stationary observer;
- the Toybox once uses **facters**, apparently a typo for faceters. Prefer **faceters**.

## Foulmaxxing / foulmaxing

**Status:** Core and Toybox canon; colloquial term

Extreme body modification meant to manipulate an observer’s fear, disgust, and desire to harm so that the Zoo hides the modified person. Modifications include implanted technologies, animal characteristics, open wounds, sickness signals, extra limbs or eyes, and target-specific fear cues. [Z0, Z1–Z3, Z5, Z6, C]

The stories use both **foulmaxxing** and **foulmaxing**; the official Toybox prefers **Foulmaxxing**. Practitioners are **foulmaxxers** or **foulmaxers**.

## Phobisculpting / Phobisculting

**Status:** Core and Toybox canon; technical term; Continuity caution

The formal or technical label for foulmaxxing. Chapter 2 and the Codex use **phobisculpting**; the Toybox uses **Phobisculting**. Prefer **phobisculpting** when echoing the story and **Foulmaxxing (Phobisculting)** when echoing the submission Toybox.

## Distinction: faceting versus foulmaxxing

Professor Reynolds’ formulation:

- **Foulmaxxers hack feelings.**
- **Faceters hack the protocols.**

Their practical result can be the same—disappearance on one’s own terms—but their methods and philosophies differ. [Z5]

# XI. Highest-value interoperability findings

## 1. The substitute render has a strong canonical rhyme

Zoothesia makes the same document appear differently to different observers. Mirage Handler replaces hidden files with abundant, harmless information rather than suspicious blankness. The paired renders therefore sit neatly between the two systems:

- **Zoothesia contribution:** observer-specific perceptual access;
- **Trainverse contribution:** innocuous information substituted for suppressed information;
- **artifact invention:** a municipal coating bulletin occupying the exact geometry of a classified intelligence bulletin.

This connection should remain structural. Naming Mirage Handler inside the bulletin would make the mechanism feel borrowed rather than independently emergent.

## 2. Maintenance labor is already a crossover pressure point

Fu Kenan is a maintenance worker whose overlay turns residue into attention demands and whose manipulation of surfaces exhausts the classifier. SECOND COAT must therefore remain distinct:

- Fu deliberately loads surfaces with private mnemonic imagery until they disappear.
- SECOND COAT plants minimal seeds so that **municipal abatement itself** produces the larger, public, official signal.

The difference is strong enough, but the bulletin should not add memory palaces, densely symbol-covered bodies, or compute saturation as SECOND COAT’s primary mechanism.

## 3. The two surveillance systems must remain separate

- **The Zoo** decides what an observer perceives.
- **ECOROUTE** continuously tracks biometric and compliance status on the train.
