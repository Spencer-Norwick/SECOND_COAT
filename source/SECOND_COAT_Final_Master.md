# RESIDUAL STAINING FOLLOWING COMPLIANT NEUTRALIZATION OF UNAUTHORIZED MARKINGS

*Guidance for the treatment of unauthorized markings on civic surfaces*

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Department has reviewed 63 reports of residual discoloration after neutralization of unauthorized markings in Chitram Row, Nayanaport, and Shraville. Even small markings may require large treatment fields: primer rectangles, replacement panels, barricades. Solvent blooms, curing overlays, and differences among permitted overlay settings may exaggerate these temporary effects.

Complaints often arise where permitted overlays render the same surface differently, causing confusion. Staff should document only physical treatments performed. Avoid speculative descriptions of unauthorized markings which can encourage imitative defacement and generate unnecessary public contact.

Public reports describing the traces as intentional symbols remain unsupported. For administrative clarity, **SECOND COAT** denotes only the finishing pass in official recoating procedures, not an organization. Rapid, standardized treatment remains the preferred means of restoring an appropriate civic surface.

## FIELD PROCEDURE

1. **SURVEY.** Log surface type, grit, unauthorized marking, pressure marks, or reflectance anomalies.
2. **LIFT.** Remove any loose material; log the interval before crew arrival.
3. **NEUTRALIZE.** Select and apply treatment according to surface, coating age, overlay permissions, and inventory availability.
4. **READBACK.** Inspect and document treatment dimensions, barricade orientation, and work-order prefix as well as any solvent bloom or residual staining.
5. **RECOAT.** Apply a second, finishing coat only when reflectance remains uneven.

Field terms: **ghost**, residual staining after cleaning; **split**, differing appearance across overlay settings.

## CASE NOTE // CHITRAM ROW PUMP STATION 6B

A 14-centimeter unauthorized marking on the north-facing exterior wall required a 0.05-square-meter primer field and an eleven-hour curing overlay. Seventeen pedestrians approached; six made physical contact with the surface, while eleven detoured around the public-safety barricades.

Two days later, a similar marking appeared beneath the rail viaduct. Its overlay signature matched the Pump Station treatment. Department review attributes the similarity to standardized treatment procedure and crew overlap.

## MATERIALS FINDINGS

- Standardization improves training efficacy and inventory control.
- Procedure deviations increase appearance of treatment residues.
- Treatment delay increases surface penetration of unauthorized marking; non-treatment increases public contact.
- Overlay-only masking does not correct underlying defacement and leads to increased overlay splits.

No known method eliminates all perceived variation across all substrates and permitted overlays.

## HANDLING / JUDGMENT NOTES

Fresh coatings may appear blank before fully curing. Do not alter crew routes, barricade orientation, or overlay notices without prior departmental approval. Route repeat incidents through the original work order. Technicians logging more than three repeat incidents at the same site should be rotated to a new route.

**No underlying signaling mechanism is indicated. Continue normal treatment procedures.**

## PUBLIC INFORMATION

Treatment fields are temporary maintenance records. Do not photograph, trace, or reproduce them.
